AI as we know it has existed longer than most of us have originally believed. Created in 1966 by Joseph Weizenbaum, ELIZA is essentially the first AI chatbot program to be made, designed to process and ingage in conversations with Humans. Eliza mainly operated using predefined scripts that would be selected through pattern matching. Its most famous script the "Doctor" was made to simulate a therapist. For example "User: I am worried about my job. - ELIZA: What does your job mean to you?" (1) As seen here it was used to encourage the user to self reflect rather than ELIZA providing actual meaningful responses.

In 1972, years after the creation of ELIZA, a psychiatrist named Kenneth Colby developed a new chatbot named PARRY. Colbys aim when making PARRY was for the chatbot to not only go beyond making basic replies but to also emulate the paranoia of a mentally troubled person. The main reason Colby designed PARRY like this was to create engaging conversations with users and other practitioners that were wondering about the possibility of seperating psychotherapy from AI. In 1973 an experiment was ran on wether psychiatrists could tell the difference in responses between PARRY and patients. The results attracted much attention due to the inconsistant reliability of the psychiatrists judgements, and inturn a major question was asked "when behaviour appears convincing, how do we know what lies behind it?"
